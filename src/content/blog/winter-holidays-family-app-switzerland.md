October is here, which means one thing for Swiss families: winter is coming, and with it come school ski weeks, Advent calendars, Christmas market evenings, and the annual scramble to coordinate time off between two working parents. Switzerland's winter calendar is packed — and if you've ever tried to manage a family ski trip while also tracking a child's Wednesday sports practice and a shared Christmas gift list, you know exactly how quickly things fall apart without a system.

## Why Winter Is the Hardest Season to Plan as a Family

Summer has a long, predictable break. Winter is different. Between November and February, Swiss families navigate:

- **Sportwoche (school ski week):** Each canton runs these on different weeks, and for families with children in different cantons — or simply in different school levels — the dates rarely line up.
- **Advent and Christmas:** Gift lists, school concerts, festive dinners with grandparents and extended family, charity drives, and a dozen small traditions that somehow all land in December.
- **Fasnacht / Carnival** (Basel, Lucerne, and many cantons): costume planning, event tickets, and travel logistics.
- **Winterferien:** Most cantons have a week off in January or February. Coordinating this with both parents' work schedules is rarely simple.
- **New Year's plans:** Often the most last-minute, highest-stress piece of the puzzle.

None of these are large in isolation. Together, they create weeks where every day has a moving part and someone is always asking "but what are we doing on the 23rd?"

## Switzerland's Best Winter Activities for Families with Kids

### Ski and Snow

Switzerland has some of Europe's best family skiing. A few standouts worth booking early:

- **Flumserberg (SG):** One of the most family-friendly ski resorts in eastern Switzerland. Ski school for children from age 3. Easy access from Zurich.
- **Hoch-Ybrig (SZ):** Small, affordable, and rarely overcrowded. Ideal for a first family ski day.
- **Laax (GR):** Larger terrain, excellent snowpark for teenagers, and a well-run children's ski school.
- **Saas-Fee (VS):** Glacier skiing almost guarantees snow. Popular with families from the French-speaking part of Switzerland.
- **Engelberg (OW):** A classic, with the Titlis cable car making it worthwhile even on a non-ski day.

**Practical tip:** Book ski school places and rental equipment at least six to eight weeks before your trip. Popular resorts fill up for Sportwoche weeks by November.

### Non-Ski Winter Days

Not every day in winter needs skis. Some of Switzerland's best family winter activities don't involve a mountain:

- **Christmas markets:** Basel, Zurich, Bern, and Montreux each run large Weihnachtsmärkte from late November. The Montreux Christmas market, set along Lake Geneva, is particularly spectacular.
- **Ice skating:** Lausanne, Zurich, Bern, and dozens of smaller towns set up outdoor rinks from November. Free or very low-cost.
- **Schlitteln (sledding):** Many Swiss resorts have illuminated sledging runs. The Prätschli run in Arosa and the Rodelbahn in Grindelwald are family favourites.
- **Indoor days:** The Swiss Museum of Transport in Lucerne, Kindermuseum Creaviva in Bern, and the Technorama in Winterthur all make excellent bad-weather options.
- **Advent activities:** Many Swiss communities organise Adventsfenster (advent window trails), Samichlaus visits (December 6th), and lantern walks in the first week of December.

## Planning the Swiss Sportwoche: A Practical Checklist

The Sportwoche is often the most logistically complex event of the winter. Here's what to plan — use this as the basis of a shared task list in Eazy.Family:

- [ ] Confirm Sportwoche dates for each child's school
- [ ] Book accommodation (chalets book out fast — start in October)
- [ ] Arrange ski equipment rental or check what fits from last year
- [ ] Renew or purchase ski passes (season passes often offer better value)
- [ ] Check if a medical fitness certificate is required
- [ ] Pack ski bag: helmet, goggles, warm layers, sunscreen (altitude sun is strong even in winter)
- [ ] Add emergency contacts and resort first aid number to the family app
- [ ] Coordinate pick-up and drop-off if children travel with a school group

For families with [working parents coordinating childcare](/resources/blog/family-app-working-parents), the Sportwoche often also means one parent takes time off while the other doesn't — another reason a shared calendar helps both partners see the full picture.

## Advent and Christmas: Managing the Busy Season as a Family

December in Switzerland is wonderful and relentless. A few ways a family app helps:

**Gift list coordination.** A shared list where both parents (and older children) can add ideas, assign who is buying what, and mark items as purchased. This avoids duplicate gifts and last-minute panic.

**Logistics for family gatherings.** Christmas with extended family across multiple households often involves travel, overnight stays, and catering coordination. One shared calendar entry with the address, arrival time, and what each family is bringing keeps it simple.

**Advent calendar and traditions.** Many Swiss families maintain cherished Advent traditions — opening a new book each night, a specific walk to a local Christmas market, the Samichlaus evening. Adding these to a recurring calendar keeps them from getting squeezed out by December's relentless pace.

**Travel.** Whether you're heading to a ski resort for the week between Christmas and New Year, visiting grandparents in another canton, or taking a short city trip, having all travel details (booking confirmations, train times, hotel addresses) in a shared family space means no one is searching through email at 6am on a departure day.
