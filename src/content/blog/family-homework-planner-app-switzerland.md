September in Switzerland means new school years, fresh exercise books, and — for most families with children — the return of the homework challenge. One child has a maths test to prepare for, another has a book report due Friday, and somehow both of them managed to leave the relevant worksheets at school. Meanwhile, the family calendar is already packed with swimming lessons, dental check-ups, and a birthday party on Saturday.

A **family homework planner app** does not replace the teacher or do the studying. What it does is solve the coordination layer: making sure the right person knows what is due, when it is due, and whether it has been done. For Swiss families juggling multiple children across different school years and different cantonal schedules, that coordination layer matters more than many parents expect.

## Why Homework Coordination Is a Family Problem, Not Just a Child Problem

In Swiss primary schools, homework starts in the second or third grade, depending on the canton. By the time a child reaches the Mittelstufe or lower secondary level, weekly assignments, reading tasks, and project deadlines are a regular fixture. For two-parent working households — which make up a large share of families in Zurich, Basel, and Geneva — there is a recurring challenge: the parent who picks up the child often does not know what homework was assigned, and the child's verbal summary is rarely complete.

The result is a familiar pattern: homework surfaces at 8 pm, when no one has the energy for it. Or a project deadline appears without warning the week before. Or a child learns to quietly skip assignments that do not get checked.

The problem is not motivation or discipline. It is information flow. Homework assignments live in the child's head or on a paper planner that rarely makes it home intact. Without a shared system, parents cannot support what they cannot see.

A family homework planner app changes that by creating a transparent, shared record — one that works on the family's devices rather than on the school's communication tools.

## What to Look for in a Family Homework Planner App

Not every task management tool is suited to homework tracking. A tool built for adults in a workplace context has different defaults — urgency, accountability, and completion metrics that do not translate well to a ten-year-old finishing a reading assignment.

Here is what actually helps in a family context:

### Simple entry that children can manage themselves

If entering homework into the app takes longer than writing it in a paper planner, it will not happen. The best systems let children add a task in a few taps — subject, what is due, when. Parents can review and add context or reminders without taking over the process.

### Reminders at the right time

A reminder about a test the morning of the test is useless. A reminder two days before, at a time when the family is actually at home, is what changes behaviour. A good family homework planner lets parents set reminders that go to both the parent and the child's device, at a time that actually allows preparation.

### Integration with the family calendar

Homework deadlines belong in the same view as music lessons, football practice, and family commitments. If a child has a football tournament the weekend before a big project is due, that needs to be visible together — not in two separate apps. [A shared family calendar](/resources/blog/family-calendar-app-switzerland) that combines activities and school deadlines solves this problem at the family level.

### Separate task lists per child

A family with three children has three sets of homework — and they do not mix. A good system keeps each child's assignments separate while still giving parents an overview across all children. This is one area where a generic task app often fails: it is built for a single user's tasks, not for a household managing multiple students.

### Transparency without micromanagement

The goal is not to surveil every homework session. It is to give parents enough visibility to support their children before a crisis, without hovering. A completion status — done, in progress, not started — that children update themselves builds accountability and reduces the need for daily interrogation.

## How Swiss School Schedules Make This Harder

Switzerland's cantonal education system means that no two cantons have identical school structures. Zurich's Volksschule runs differently from Geneva's system, which differs again from what families experience in Ticino or Bern. Children can be in the same family and attending schools with different term dates, half-day arrangements, and homework expectations.

International families in Zurich or Geneva — many of whom use international schools running on UK, US, or IB curricula — face an additional layer of difference. The school's own communication app (if there is one) is often separate from the family's primary planning tools.

The [school holidays in Switzerland](/resources/blog/school-holidays-switzerland-app) vary significantly by canton, which means that different children in the same household can have different break dates. A family homework planner that sits alongside a shared family calendar makes these discrepancies visible and manageable rather than a source of weekly confusion.

## Turning Homework Chaos Into a Manageable Routine

The shift from reactive homework management to proactive planning rarely comes from a single conversation. It comes from a reliable system that is simple enough to actually use every day.

For children who are old enough to track their own assignments, the act of entering homework into a shared system builds a habit of planning ahead. The parent does not need to ask "do you have homework?" every day. The system shows what is in progress, and the parent can engage from a position of knowing rather than guessing.
