Running a household is a management job. It involves scheduling, delegation, communication, supply chain (the shopping), and a never-ending backlog of recurring tasks. Most families do all of this across a combination of mental notes, a WhatsApp group, a shared Google calendar that one parent maintains, and sticky notes on the fridge.

It works — until someone is travelling, or a school email gets missed, or two people show up to pick up the same child because neither checked.

A household management app is the tool that replaces the patchwork. If you live in Switzerland and are looking for one, this post covers exactly what to look for and why the generic options often fall short for Swiss families.

## What "Household Management" Actually Means

The phrase sounds corporate, but the reality is practical. Household management covers:

- **Scheduling** — keeping track of who needs to be where, when, including school events, sports, medical appointments, and family activities
- **Task delegation** — assigning recurring and one-off tasks to the right person (including children, when age-appropriate)
- **Shopping and supplies** — maintaining shared lists that everyone can update in real time
- **Communication** — keeping family members informed without requiring a separate message for every update
- **Memory** — storing information that someone will need later: school contact numbers, renewal dates, medication schedules, upcoming birthdays

A household management app brings all of this into one place, visible to everyone in the family who needs to see it.

## Why Swiss Families Need a Different Approach

Switzerland adds specific layers of complexity that generic household apps — most of which were designed for American or UK markets — do not handle well.

**Cantonal school calendars.** Swiss school holidays vary significantly by canton. Zurich, Geneva, and Ticino all follow different calendars, and families that span cantons (common for those who work in one canton and live in another) manage multiple schedules simultaneously. A household management app useful in Switzerland needs to make cantonal calendar differences visible, not assume a single national school year.

**Multilingualism.** Switzerland has four national languages. Many Swiss households include parents who grew up in different linguistic regions, or who moved from abroad. A household management app that only operates in English — or only in German — immediately excludes part of the family. For the app to work, everyone needs to be able to use it in their own language.

**Privacy standards.** Swiss data protection law (DSG, revised in 2023) and European GDPR both set high standards for how personal data may be collected and processed. Swiss families are generally more privacy-conscious than users in other markets. A household management app that monetises family data through advertising is a difficult sell in Switzerland, where the norm is transparency about how data is used.

**Multilingual school communications.** In Geneva and Fribourg, school communications arrive in French. In Bern, in German. In Lugano, in Italian. In international schools across all cantons, in English. A Swiss family's household management tool needs to work alongside whatever language the school uses, not fight it.

## What a Household Management App for Swiss Families Should Include

### A shared family calendar that actually syncs

The calendar is the foundation of household management. It needs to show all family members' schedules in one view, send reminders before events, and support real-time updates when plans change. For Swiss families, cantonal school holiday templates are a meaningful addition — loading the correct holiday calendar for your canton saves significant manual work at the start of each school year.

### Task and chore management

A good household management app includes task lists with assignees and due dates. This is different from a shared calendar: a calendar shows *when*, a task list shows *what* and *who*. For families with children, being able to assign age-appropriate tasks — and for children to mark them done themselves — replaces the negotiation that happens every Saturday morning about who is cleaning what.

### A shared shopping list

The shopping list is the most-used household management feature for most families. It should update in real time (so adding an item from the office means the partner picking up groceries can see it immediately), support categorisation (produce, dairy, cleaning supplies), and be accessible without requiring the other person to open a specific app. A link-based access option matters for this feature.

### Family messaging in one place

Household management that relies on a separate WhatsApp group for coordination is halfway there. The messages about the household need to be connected to the household context — a message about tomorrow's school pickup is more useful when it sits next to tomorrow's calendar event, not buried in a chat thread between emoji reactions to someone's holiday photo.

### Child access without a separate phone

Children are household members too, and including them in household management — as participants, not just subjects — works better when they can see and update their own tasks and schedule. A good household management app supports child profiles that a parent controls, without requiring the child to have their own phone number, email address, or app account.

## Making the Switch: What to Expect

Switching from an improvised household management system (the WhatsApp group, the shared Google calendar, the fridge notepad) to a dedicated app takes one or two weeks to feel natural. The main phases are:

1. **Import the calendar.** Most household management apps support importing from Google Calendar or iCal. Getting the existing schedule in from the start prevents the app from feeling empty in the first week.

2. **Get everyone set up.** The app only works if everyone is using it. The barrier needs to be low: onboarding that takes more than five minutes will lose family members before they start. Eazy.Family onboarding is web-based — no app download required to start.

3. **Transfer the active shopping list.** Moving the current shopping list into the shared app list on day one makes it immediately useful, rather than maintaining two lists in parallel during a transition period.

4. **Assign the recurring tasks.** Setting up the weekly and monthly recurring tasks once, with the right assignees, means the chore-negotiation conversation largely stops happening.

The payoff for most families is visible within two weeks: fewer "did you see my message?" conversations, no more double-bookings because two people checked different calendars, and a single source of truth for what needs to happen and who is doing it.
